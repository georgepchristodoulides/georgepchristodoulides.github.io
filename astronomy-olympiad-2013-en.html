1st Olympiad · Cyprus, 2013

Astronomy and Astrophysics: 50 Questions and Answers

These are the questions George Christodoulides wrote for the first Astronomy Olympiad in Cyprus, in 2013.

The questions and answers

Select a question to hide or show its answer.

  1. 1.Is the Universe static, expanding or contracting?

    Answer: The Universe is expanding and, according to observations, its expansion is accelerating.

  2. 2.What is the estimated age of the Universe?

    Answer: Approximately 13.8 billion years.

  3. 3.Can we observe the entire Universe? Why?

    Answer: No. We can observe only the observable Universe, because light from more distant regions has not had enough time to reach us.

  4. 4.Approximately how long does light take to travel from the Moon to Earth?

    Answer: Approximately 1.3 seconds.

  5. 5.What are the four fundamental interactions of nature?

    Answer: Gravity, electromagnetism, the strong nuclear interaction and the weak nuclear interaction.

  6. 6.According to the standard cosmological model, what percentage of the Universe’s total matter and energy density is currently made up of ordinary, baryonic matter?

    Answer: Approximately 5%. The remainder consists mainly of dark matter and dark energy.

  7. 7.How many years after the Big Bang did recombination occur, when neutral atoms formed and the Universe became transparent to light?

    Answer: Approximately 380,000 years after the Big Bang.

  8. 8.Name two types of gravitational lensing.

    Answer: Strong gravitational lensing and weak gravitational lensing.

  9. 9.Which theory of physics explains gravitational lensing?

    Answer: Einstein’s general theory of relativity.

  10. 10.Name one physical mechanism that produces X-rays.

    Answer: Bremsstrahlung, or braking radiation: electrons emit radiation when they are deflected or slowed down in the electric fields of atomic nuclei.

  11. 11.Why do X-ray astronomical observations require instruments carried by satellites, rockets or high-altitude balloons, rather than telescopes on Earth’s surface?

    Answer: Because Earth’s atmosphere absorbs X-rays before they reach the ground.

  12. 12.Which chemical element accounts for most of the mass of ordinary matter in the Universe?

    Answer: Hydrogen, which accounts for approximately three-quarters of this mass.

  13. 13.What is the name of the galaxy that contains our Solar System?

    Answer: The Milky Way.

  14. 14.What is the nearest large spiral galaxy to the Milky Way?

    Answer: The Andromeda Galaxy, approximately 2.5 million light-years away.

  15. 15.Which light has a longer wavelength in a vacuum: red or blue?

    Answer: Red light.

  16. 16.What is the estimated age of Earth?

    Answer: Approximately 4.54 billion years.

  17. 17.According to the leading scientific hypothesis, how did the Moon form?

    Answer: From material ejected into orbit when a body approximately the size of Mars collided with the early Earth.

  18. 18.Which observations can provide information about the state of the Universe before the epoch of recombination?

    Answer: Primarily the small fluctuations in the cosmic microwave background, which preserve imprints of earlier conditions.

  19. 19.Which criterion in the definition of a planet does Pluto fail to meet, resulting in its classification as a dwarf planet?

    Answer: It has not become gravitationally dominant in the neighbourhood of its orbit by removing or controlling other comparable bodies.

  20. 20.Write the relationship between the fractional change in the wavelength of light due to the Doppler effect and the source’s radial velocity relative to the observer, when this velocity is much smaller than the speed of light.

    Answer: Δλ/λ₀ ≈ vᵣ/c, where λ₀ is the rest wavelength and vᵣ is the radial velocity, taken as positive when the source is moving away.

  21. 21.What is the speed of light in a vacuum?

    Answer: Exactly 299,792,458 m/s, or approximately 300,000 km/s.

  22. 22.What is the approximate speed of sound in air at a temperature of 20 °C?

    Answer: Approximately 343 m/s.

  23. 23.What is the temperature of absolute zero in degrees Celsius and in kelvins?

    Answer: −273.15 °C, or 0 K.

  24. 24.What type of nuclear reaction produces the energy emitted by the Sun?

    Answer: Nuclear fusion, primarily the conversion of hydrogen into helium through the proton–proton chain.

  25. 25.What is the largest planet in the Solar System?

    Answer: Jupiter.

  26. 26.What is the smallest planet in the Solar System?

    Answer: Mercury.

  27. 27.Why is the gravitational acceleration at the Moon’s surface smaller than at Earth’s surface?

    Answer: Because the ratio of its mass to the square of its radius is smaller. The relationship g = GM/R² gives a surface gravitational acceleration about one-sixth of Earth’s.

  28. 28.What is a binary star system?

    Answer: Two stars that are gravitationally bound and orbit their common centre of mass.

  29. 29.An optical telescope and a radio telescope have the same diameter. If their resolution is limited only by diffraction, which has better angular resolution, and why?

    Answer: The optical telescope, because it observes at shorter wavelengths. The minimum resolvable angle is proportional to λ/D.

  30. 30.If we viewed the Solar System from the north ecliptic pole, would the planets orbit the Sun clockwise or anticlockwise? Are there any exceptions to their direction of orbital motion?

    Answer: All eight planets orbit anticlockwise. There are no exceptions to this direction of orbital motion around the Sun.

  31. 31.State Kepler’s first law.

    Answer: Planets move in elliptical orbits, with the Sun at one focus of each ellipse.

  32. 32.State Heisenberg’s uncertainty principle for a particle’s position and momentum.

    Answer: The uncertainties in position and the corresponding component of momentum satisfy Δx·Δpₓ ≥ ℏ/2.

  33. 33.Give one example of an application of the uncertainty principle in astrophysics.

    Answer: Estimating the kinetic energy of electrons in white dwarfs. Together with the Pauli exclusion principle, it helps explain degeneracy pressure.

  34. 34.A star has a surface temperature of 10,000 K. Assuming that it radiates as a blackbody, calculate the wavelength at which its emitted power per unit wavelength is greatest.

    Answer: Wien’s displacement law gives λmax = 2.898 × 10⁻³/T ≈ 290 nm, in the ultraviolet region.

  35. 35.Through which fundamental interactions do neutrinos interact?

    Answer: Through the weak nuclear interaction and gravity.

  36. 36.How do cold and hot dark matter differ in terms of their particle speeds in the early Universe and their effects on the formation of cosmic structures?

    Answer: Cold dark matter particles moved relatively slowly when structures began to form. Hot dark matter particles moved much faster, smoothing out small concentrations of matter and making it harder for small structures to form.

  37. 37.What information do the dark absorption lines in a star’s spectrum provide, and why do they appear dark?

    Answer: They reveal the star’s chemical composition and provide information about its temperature and motion. They appear dark because gas in the star’s atmosphere selectively absorbs light at particular wavelengths.

  38. 38.During the overall process of hydrogen fusion into helium in the Sun, approximately 0.7% of the initial mass is converted into energy. Which equation relates the decrease in mass to the energy released?

    Answer: E = Δm·c², where Δm is the decrease in mass and c is the speed of light in a vacuum.

  39. 39.Why are far more visible impact craters preserved on the Moon than on Earth?

    Answer: Because the Moon has almost no atmospheric erosion, running water or active plate tectonics, all of which erase many of Earth’s craters.

  40. 40.Which physical quantities are plotted on the two axes of a Hertzsprung–Russell diagram?

    Answer: Usually surface temperature on the horizontal axis and luminosity on the vertical axis. Temperature decreases towards the right.

  41. 41.What is the difference between a star’s luminosity and its apparent brightness?

    Answer: Luminosity is the total energy the star emits per second. Apparent brightness is the energy we receive per second per unit area, and it also depends on the star’s distance.

  42. 42.Why can Cepheid variable stars be used to measure astronomical distances?

    Answer: Because their pulsation period is related to their luminosity. Comparing this luminosity with their apparent brightness allows us to calculate their distance.

  43. 43.How does a neutron star form?

    Answer: Usually from the collapse of a massive star’s core during a supernova explosion, when the remnant is not massive enough to become a black hole.

  44. 44.What is the approximate upper limit on the initial mass of a star that can end its life as a white dwarf?

    Answer: Approximately eight solar masses, with the exact limit depending on the star’s composition and evolution.

  45. 45.What diameter must a radio telescope observing at a wavelength of 1 cm have to achieve the same angular resolution as an optical telescope with a diameter of 10 cm observing at a wavelength of 500 nm? Assume that both telescopes are limited only by diffraction.

    Answer: Setting λᵣ/Dᵣ = λₒ/Dₒ gives Dᵣ = 2,000 m, or a diameter of 2 km.

  46. 46.What order of magnitude of temperature is required in a star’s core for helium fusion to produce carbon and oxygen?

    Answer: A temperature of the order of 100 million kelvins, or approximately 10⁸ K.

  47. 47.Write the Stefan–Boltzmann law for the total power emitted by a blackbody per unit surface area.

    Answer: P/A = σT⁴, where T is the absolute temperature and σ ≈ 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W·m⁻²·K⁻⁴.

  48. 48.Name one theoretical approach that attempts to explain galaxy rotation curves by modifying the laws of dynamics or gravity, rather than assuming the presence of dark matter.

    Answer: MOND, or Modified Newtonian Dynamics, which proposes changes to dynamics at very small accelerations.

  49. 49.Name a radio telescope or an array of radio telescopes.

    Answer: The ALMA array in Chile.

  50. 50.Name a telescope that is planned or under construction.

    Answer: The European Southern Observatory’s Extremely Large Telescope (ELT), which is under construction in Chile.
